What is simulated?

A single spatially periodic (toroidal) two-population E/I lattice is simulated; the left/right edges and top/bottom edges are identified through wraparound, while synaptic interactions remain spatially local, not all-to-all. The Torus view depicts the periodic topology. The Sphere view is an artistic texture mapping with different geometry. A recurrent two-population E/I lattice is simulated with your choice of LIF, adaptive LIF (with spike-triggered recovery), or Izhikevich neurons (regular-spiking E and fast-spiking I). All three produce discrete spikes and drive spatial E→E, E→I, I→E and I→I interactions. Recurrent synaptic fields can be delayed using a characteristic-distance conduction approximation. The central perturbation is an added Gaussian external input to excitatory neurons, with adjustable intensity, spread and pulse duration. The Brunel-balanced preset is a Brunel-inspired inhibition-dominated heuristic with stochastic external drive inside this equal-sized spatial E/I lattice, not a faithful Brunel sparse random network or proof of a balanced asynchronous irregular state.

How is the artwork made?

Colors encode exponentially smoothed excitatory spike traces. Palette, hue, contrast and 3D projection alter the rendering only. The 3D surface, rolled sheet, sphere and torus visualize the same 2D numerical field; none is a neuronal anatomical reconstruction. Only the torus depicts both periodic identifications topologically. The pulse marker is an overlay, while stimulation directly enters neuronal dynamics. Results depend on the model, coupling, stimulus history and random seed. This is an exploratory periodic-lattice SNN, not a validated biological model or proof of a mathematical Turing instability.
