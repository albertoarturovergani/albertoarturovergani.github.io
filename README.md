# Alberto Arturo Vergani — personal academic website

Public site: **https://albertoarturovergani.github.io**

This is a lightweight static site hosted by GitHub Pages. It includes the complete CV structure maintained as the academic web CV (November 2026), a search-ready bibliography from `vergani_scholar.bib`, and scientific Brain Art projects. No portrait or personal signature is included.

## Pages

- `index.html` — homepage with research areas, latest news (events, videos and publications), academic path, **all four education qualifications**, recognition, science/art and contact links.
- `cv.html` — **full academic CV**, with the same section structure as the research CV: Introduction; Prizes, Proposals and Fellowships; Academic Information (Positions, Collaborations, Visiting periods, Teaching, Tutoring, Commissions, Memberships, Chairing, Event Organization, Journals Engagement, References, Education); Presentations (Talks, Posters, Seminars); Continuous learning (Workshops, Schools, Courses); Extra Information (Programming skills, Third mission, other academic and non-academic activities); Neuroscience Art; Scientific Outcomes.
- `publications.html` — complete BibTeX-based bibliography, filterable by **topic, publication type and year**, with text search. Topics can overlap.
- `neural-turing-lab.html` — interactive Neural Turing Patterns SNN laboratory, linked from Brain Art, with **51,200 neurons (160 × 160 E and 160 × 160 I)** and **Sharp / Smooth** display modes. It offers four spiking neuron models (LIF, adaptive LIF, Izhikevich and Hodgkin–Huxley), six presets (**Spots, Labyrinth, Islands, Traveling waves, Spiral waves, Brunel-balanced**), adjustable E→E / E→I / I→E / I→I coupling, local and inhibitory radii, delay, characteristic conduction speed, and a Gaussian central stimulation input. It also includes visualization-only 2D / 3D height-map / rolled-sheet modes, rotatable 3D projection, relief and roll controls, and palette / hue / contrast adjustments; the PNG export reflects the selected view, and JSON records the display settings.
  - Traveling and spiral wave presets use an **explicitly moving or rotating spatial drive** injected into the recurrent E/I spiking lattice; the visible activity is generated by neuron spikes rather than pre-rendered textures. Neither preset establishes self-sustaining autonomous waves. Brunel-balanced is a **Brunel-inspired** higher-inhibition, stochastic-drive regime in an equal E/I lattice, not a reproduction of Brunel's random sparsely connected 4:1 E/I network or a verified asynchronous irregular/balanced state.
  - Below the pattern are a **5-second nominal rolling Welch mean PSD on log-log axes from 1 to 500 nominal Hz**, a Morlet time–frequency scalogram, a real-time E/I population-voltage phase plane, and E/I voltage time series. The Welch calculation uses 1024-point FFTs at 1 ms/step (Nyquist 500 nominal Hz) or 2048-point FFTs at 0.5 ms/step (Nyquist 1000 nominal Hz, chart limited to 500); Hann windows, 50% overlap. An explicitly labeled partial PSD preview appears while the 5-second history fills, with CSV export after the window is complete. The wavelet-average-power plot remains removed.
  - Conduction speed is in **lattice cells/step**, delay in **simulation steps**, and 5-second PSD uses the selectable **nominal analysis step of 1 or 0.5 ms** without changing neuronal integration. No physiological timebase or calibrated Hz is implied. Larger network and full 5-second windows are computationally demanding; the first complete PSD depends on the simulation reaching the requisite number of steps. All simulation and analysis remain in-browser with no server uploads; the generator is separate from the award-winning original artwork.

The CV page includes original content, thesis details and activities from the full CV, rather than only the abbreviated homepage timeline. Its `Scientific Outcomes` metrics are a **dated historical snapshot**, not a live feed. Bibliography counts are generated separately to avoid presenting historical counts as current. Contact information for third-party academic referees is intentionally omitted from the public page.

## Add or update a publication

1. Open `vergani_scholar.bib` in this repository, choose **Edit** and add the BibTeX record with a unique citation key, or modify an existing record.
2. If the publication status needs an explicit correction (accepted, in preparation, submitted, etc.), update `content/status.json` using the corresponding BibTeX citation key.
3. To manually assign subject tags, update `content/topics.json`, e.g. `"my2027paper": ["neuroscience", "computer-science"]`. You can use several tags for one paper. Existing records have been manually classified using their scientific topics; new records receive an automatic **suggestion** based on keywords until reviewed. Never treat the keyword fallback as an authoritative discipline classification.
4. Commit the changes. **GitHub Actions regenerates `data/publications.json` and deploys the pages**. The bibliography can be found via search or the subject links on the homepage.

Subjects use multiple overlapping labels (Neuroscience, Computer Science & AI, History of Science, Neuroengineering, Brain Modelling, Education), plus more specific tags for EEG, MRI/fMRI, Machine Learning, Cerebellum, Alzheimer’s and Cognitive Decline, Schizophrenia, Brain Stimulation/TMS, Spiking Networks, Dopamine/Basal Ganglia, and Neurodevelopment/Epilepsy. Subject counts overlap and must not be summed. Visitors can select multiple tags and match ANY (OR) or ALL (AND). **The default archive view includes all publication types, but excludes records tagged `history`**. The buttons All including history or History of Science make those records available.

New works are **not automatically harvested** from journals, Google Scholar or ORCID: add a BibTeX entry to trigger the automated publication refresh. Verify DOI, category and acceptance status. Some references in the supplied CV are labelled as submitted or planned for 2027; they are not represented as peer-reviewed publications.

### Store the abstract with each reference

Each record in `vergani_scholar.bib` has an `abstract` field. Verified original abstracts have been added where the exact source and version could be checked; remaining fields are empty. Paste the text inside the braces, keeping the trailing comma and the existing citation key. For new references, add the same field; leave it empty while the text is unavailable. The `.bib` is the single maintained source for references and abstracts. [ABSTRACT_SOURCES.md](ABSTRACT_SOURCES.md) records the sources, licenses and retrieval date; `content/abstract_sources.json` stores the same provenance with text checksums. No generated summaries substitute for missing abstracts.

See [ABSTRACTS.md](ABSTRACTS.md) for the editing guide in Italian and a direct GitHub editing link. After a commit, the existing build preserves the complete record, including the abstract, in the downloadable bibliography and **Copy BibTeX**. Publication cards display a collapsed **Abstract** panel when text is available, with original-source and license links when recorded. Verified associated repositories appear as GitHub buttons alongside Copy BibTeX.

## Associated GitHub repositories

`content/repositories.json` maps BibTeX citation keys to verified repository links. Each key contains a list of objects with `url`, `label`, `evidence_url`, `evidence` and `verified_on`. Use the article's code/data availability section or a repository README that explicitly identifies the work. Repository labels can specify a resource, such as **Atlas on GitHub** or **GitHub DADD**, when the repository covers that resource rather than every analysis in the paper.

The build validates citation keys and GitHub repository URLs, then includes the links in `data/publications.json`. Cards display these links independently of abstract availability. An absent mapping produces no repository button. Add new abstracts to the `.bib` and new verified repository associations to this mapping; the existing GitHub Pages build refreshes both.

## Deployment

This repository is public and contains `.github/workflows/pages.yml`. In **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source**, select **GitHub Actions**. The workflow runs on every `main` commit and manually through **Actions → Publish academic website → Run workflow**. The workflow needs `pages: write` and `id-token: write`, both already declared.

GitHub Pages also supports a branch-based publish option, but for **automatic BibTeX updates** use the provided GitHub Actions workflow (otherwise you need to run `python3 scripts/build_data.py` and commit the JSON output yourself).

To preview locally without extra Python dependencies:

```bash
python3 scripts/build_data.py
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Open `http://localhost:8000`.

On narrow devices the same keywords are presented as accessible clickable chips instead of shrinking the SVG text.

## November 2026: Obsidian website and topology visualizations

The homepage, Full CV and Publications share a high-contrast black-background theme. Interactive Neural Turing Patterns remains a separate self-contained dark lab.

The neural field has periodic boundary conditions on both X and Y of its E/I lattice (a two-torus). The Torus 3D projection visualizes these identifications; Sphere is an illustrative longitude/latitude activity mapping and is **not** an equivalent network topology. These renderings do not alter the SNN topology or dynamics.

## Single-compartment Hodgkin–Huxley

The interactive SNN has a fourth membrane option, classical HH with per-cell V, m, h and n and Na⁺/K⁺/leak conductances (C_m=1 µF/cm², gNa=120, gK=36, gL=0.3 mS/cm², ENa=50 mV, EK=-77 mV, EL=-54.387 mV). Two 0.05-ms membrane substeps are integrated for each network step (0.10 ms). Voltage is integrated by forward Euler and gates by exponential Euler using a precomputed 0.25-mV rate table; action potentials are detected at upward 0-mV crossing with no artificial reset. HH event-triggered traces are filtered with 6-ms decay. The original E/I coupling sliders are converted heuristically to membrane current, so different model presets and rates are not fitted or directly calibrated across neuron models. The PSD's selectable nominal Δt remains separate from HH integration time.

## Network connectivity modes

The Neural Turing Patterns interactive lab supports four options:

- **Local / toroidal:** Original 160×160 excitatory and inhibitory spatial lattice with periodic boundary conditions and local E/I field convolutions.
- **Small-world:** 256 mesoscale regions as nodes of an undirected Watts–Strogatz ring network. Tunable initial ring degree `K` (even, 2–24), independent edge rewiring probability `β` (0–1), and regional coupling mix.
- **Random (Erdős–Rényi):** Independent undirected G(256,p) edges between distinct regions, with `p` from 0 to 1 and adjustable regional coupling mix. The realized edge count and empirical density are displayed and exported; random graph construction is seed-reproducible.
- **Soft winner-take-all:** Competitive suppression of excitatory drive outside the most active top-`K` regions, with tunable winner count and competition strength; it is a dynamical motif, **not** a graph topology.

**Scale and interpretation:** There are still 51,200 neurons (25,600 E and 25,600 I) represented on the 160×160 E/I spatial lattice. Each of the 256 graph nodes aggregates 100 E and 100 I neurons within a 10×10 patch. Random and small-world inter-region connections combine region-average delayed E/I traces with local spatial fields, without enumerating individual synapses. Local toroidal wraparound persists in every mode. Therefore these modes are neither a 51,200-neuron Erdős–Rényi connectome nor a faithful Brunel 2000 random graph. The torus 3D view illustrates only local PBC, not long-range region graph edges. All graph parameters and realized edge statistics are recorded in the downloadable JSON.
